Sub-Workflows

Real systems get big. The cure is composition: build small, testable workflows and nest them. workflow.as_agent() turns an entire workflow into an AIAgent, so it can be a single participant inside a larger workflow. You'll build the Nova publishing pipeline, where a self-contained research sub-workflow (gather → fact-check) becomes one step of the outer draft → research → edit flow.

The research workflow is built, tested, and reused on its own — then dropped into the outer pipeline as if it were a single agent.

1 · Build the inner research sub-workflow

It's an ordinary workflow — here a two-stage sequential pipeline. Nothing about it knows it will be nested.

main.py
import asyncio
from agent_framework import SequentialBuilder
from agent_framework.azure import AzureOpenAIChatClient


async def main() -> None:
    client = AzureOpenAIChatClient()

    # --- inner: research sub-workflow ---
    gatherer = client.create_agent(
        name="Gatherer",
        instructions="Collect 4-6 concrete facts and figures relevant to the topic.",
    )
    fact_checker = client.create_agent(
        name="FactChecker",
        instructions="Review the facts. Flag anything unverifiable; keep only what is solid.",
    )

    research_workflow = SequentialBuilder(
        participants=[gatherer, fact_checker]
    ).build()

2 · Wrap it with as_agent() and nest it

research_workflow.as_agent(name=...) exposes the whole sub-workflow as one agent. The outer SequentialBuilder treats it like any other participant — drafter, then the research step, then editor.

main.py (cont.)
    research_step = research_workflow.as_agent(name="ResearchStep")

    # --- outer: publish pipeline ---
    drafter = client.create_agent(
        name="Drafter",
        instructions="Write a rough 150-word draft on the topic. Leave [RESEARCH] gaps.",
    )
    editor = client.create_agent(
        name="Editor",
        instructions="Fold the verified facts into the draft and tighten it to publish quality.",
    )

    publish_workflow = SequentialBuilder(
        participants=[drafter, research_step, editor]
    ).build()

    result = await publish_workflow.run(
        "Write a short explainer: why latency matters for checkout conversion."
    )
    for output in result.get_outputs():
        print(output)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    asyncio.run(main())

Why latency matters for checkout conversion

Every 100 ms of added latency cuts conversion by roughly 1% — and checkout is
the most latency-sensitive step because intent is highest and patience lowest.
Studies put cart abandonment from slow pages near 70%. Trimming p95 checkout
latency from 1.8s to 0.9s typically recovers low-single-digit conversion,
which on high-volume stores is millions in annual revenue...

Why compose this way

  1. Testable units — run and validate the research sub-workflow in isolation before nesting it.
  2. Reuse — the same ResearchStep can drop into a marketing pipeline, a report builder, or a chatbot.
  3. Encapsulation — the outer pipeline neither knows nor cares that research is two agents; it sees one.
💡
Any builder nests in any builder
Because as_agent() yields a normal agent, you can nest a concurrent fan-out inside a sequential pipeline, or a sub-workflow inside a supervisor's participant list. Mix shapes freely.
🏢
Workflows as building blocks
Treat sub-workflows like microservices: a small team owns the "enrichment" workflow, another owns "compliance review," and platform teams compose them into product flows. Each ships, versions, and tests on its own cadence. This is how the capstone assembles a full enterprise system.
